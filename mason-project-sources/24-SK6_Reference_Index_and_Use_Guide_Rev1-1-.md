## 4. Use limits and authority

The current approved estimating instructions, job-specific evidence and applicable verified requirements retain their existing authority. This guide does not rewrite that hierarchy. A conflicting passage becomes a recorded comparison or verification action, not a silent override.

Use the book to explain concepts, challenge assumptions, identify omissions and propose methods for testing. Do not use it as current NZ/AU payroll, supplier, statutory, contractual or market evidence.

Before reusing a numerical example or method, check its units, definitions, geography, date, inclusions, assumptions and arithmetic. Verify any time-sensitive requirement against appropriate current evidence. Preserve the source's terminology, and label our interpretation or local adaptation separately.

Do not import generic contingency percentages, copy process-industry accuracy bands as guarantees for other project types, or treat a risk simulation as verified merely because it produces a probability curve. Any proposed application remains subject to the existing verification and risk controls.

### Retained review caution

**Reviewer observation, not a publisher-issued erratum:** Table 2.2, printed page 23 (PDF page 24), shows annual Total Benefits of $0, $75,000, $125,000 and $100,000. These sum to $300,000, while the table prints a cumulative total of $275,000. The displayed $75,000 profit and approximately 33% simple ROI are consistent with $300,000 benefits less $225,000 costs. Preserve the original unchanged and independently check the example before any reuse.

The whole book is not assigned GREEN merely because an established professional body published it. Source identity, correctness of a specific passage and suitability for a particular estimate are separate checks.

## 6. From reference to an approved improvement

Keep the distinction between retaining knowledge and approving a method. The existing Knowledge Loop states that logging evidence is not the same as promoting it to active reusable evidence.

For a material proposed improvement, record: exact chapter/page; source method or claim; our separate interpretation; the existing system treatment; the actual gap; NZ/AU or project adaptation needed; proposed test; result; and approval decision.

Possible outcomes are **already covered**, **reference only**, **candidate for testing**, **accepted through change control**, or **not suitable for this use**. Do not duplicate an existing control merely to show that the book has been used.

My recommended first application is the existing Actual Cost & Productivity Feedback workstream, using Chapter 19 as a starting reference. First inspect what already exists. Then test one useful gap against a suitable real package with its original estimate and actual quantities, hours and costs. Missing actuals remain missing; illustrative data may test mechanics but cannot validate real-world productivity.

No new workstream, implementation, rate change or approval is authorised by this guide. Any permanent change must follow the existing methodology, test, review, integration and approval process.
