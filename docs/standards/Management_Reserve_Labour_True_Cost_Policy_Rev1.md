# 1. PURPOSE

This policy defines two permanent estimating controls:

1. **How uncertainty probabilities / scenario weights are selected and used**, including the relationship between Risk Truth and a separate Management Reserve.
2. **How labour rates are converted into true direct employment cost**, including when company actuals are unavailable and external market evidence must be normalised.

The objective is the same as the wider estimating system:

> Produce the best evidence-based forecast of what the work will genuinely cost and how long it will take, using the information reasonably available at estimate date.

This policy must never be used to manipulate an estimate to reach a target price.

---

# 2. CORE PRINCIPLES

1. **50/50 is not the default probability.**
2. A probability or scenario weight must reflect the available evidence.
3. Where one outcome is more likely than another, weight it accordingly.
4. Where probability cannot be responsibly assessed, do not invent one.
5. Probability does not create a cost unless the consequence is also supportable.
6. Known and measurable consequences belong in the direct cost chain.
7. Risk Truth remains the controlling risk-reconciliation layer.
8. Management Reserve is a separate commercial adjudication provision and must not be confused with Risk Truth, contingency, overhead or profit.
9. Labour must be priced at true direct employer cost where reasonably possible, not merely the employee's headline wage/salary.
10. No cost may be included twice.

---

# 3. PROBABILITY / WEIGHTING POLICY

## 3.1 The probability question

For every material unresolved outcome, ask:

> **Based on the evidence currently available, what is the reasonable probability of each outcome occurring?**

Do not begin with 50/50.

### Examples

- If two outcomes are genuinely equally plausible and there is no evidence favouring either, **50/50 may be appropriate**.
- If the evidence modestly favours one outcome, a weighting such as **60/40** may be appropriate.
- If several independent/current sources strongly favour one outcome, a stronger weighting such as **70/30, 80/20 or similar** may be justified.
- If the evidence is too weak to distinguish probabilities, classify the probability as **UNKNOWN / RFI HOLD** rather than inventing 50/50.

These percentages are examples only. They are **not default bands**.

## 3.2 Evidence required to support weighting

Consider:

- latest drawing / specification revision;
- consultant or client direction;
- supplier or subcontractor clarification;
- number and independence of aligned evidence sources;
- design-discipline authority;
- comparable completed-project actuals;
- contractual responsibility;
- physical constructability;
- current programme logic;
- unresolved contradictions;
- age and quality of the evidence.

The strongest source does not automatically mean the newest source. Assess **authority, relevance and recency together**.

## 3.3 Probability evidence status

Classify the weighting separately from the cost consequence:

### GREEN
Probability/weight is supported by strong project-specific or reviewed historical evidence and an independent check.

### AMBER
Probability/weight is a documented professional/commercial judgement supported by some evidence but not enough to call verified.

### RED / UNKNOWN
Probability cannot be responsibly supported.

A RED/UNKNOWN probability **must not be converted into a Probability x Impact dollar merely to complete the risk register**.

# 4. WEIGHTED EXPOSURE METHODS

## 4.1 Two-outcome event

Where there are two mutually exclusive outcomes:

**Expected Exposure = Probability of adverse outcome x incremental adverse consequence**

The consequence must be the **delta against the current priced baseline**, not the entire item value.

## 4.2 Multiple mutually exclusive scenarios

Use:

**Weighted Exposure = Sum of (Scenario Probability x Scenario Incremental Consequence)**

All mutually exclusive scenario probabilities must sum to 100%.

## 4.3 Independent risks

Independent weighted exposures may be aggregated **only after checking that the cost consequences do not overlap**.

## 4.4 Correlated or overlapping risks

Do not simply add probability-weighted amounts where two risks can cause the same cost.

Use:

- an overlap group;
- a joint scenario;
- one PRIMARY cost-treatment owner; or
- a capped combined consequence.

Risk Truth duplication rules continue to govern.

## 4.5 Unknown impact

If probability can be estimated but the cost consequence cannot:

- record the probability judgement if useful;
- record the consequence as unquantified;
- keep the risk RED/AMBER as appropriate;
- do not invent a dollar;
- consider the unquantified tail during Management Reserve adjudication.

## 4.6 Unknown probability

If the consequence is known but probability cannot be supported:

- retain the full consequence as a sensitivity;
- do not assign a false expected value;
- use RFI / scenario / exclusion / qualification / Management Reserve judgement as appropriate.

# 5. RISK TRUTH VS MANAGEMENT RESERVE

## 5.1 Risk Truth

Risk Truth remains evidence-led.

Use Risk Truth for:

- known baseline treatment;
- measurable residual exposure;
- evidence-supported Probability x Impact;
- evidence-supported Scenario Weighted models;
- specialist-engine references;
- distinct residual risk that is integrated once.

Risk Truth CA01/CA02 **must not become generic project-value contingency percentages**.

## 5.2 Management Reserve

Management Reserve is a **commercial adjudication provision**.

It exists to protect the tender against:

> **aggregate residual tender-stage uncertainty and unknown-unknowns remaining after known/measurable scope, PC sums, provisional quantities, current quotations, Weather Risk, Risk Truth and other approved specialist treatments have already been included.**

Management Reserve is:

- separate from Risk Truth;
- separate from weather;
- separate from direct physical scope;
- separate from overhead;
- separate from profit;
- normally classified **PROVIDED / AMBER**;
- approved at adjudication by the authorised estimator/commercial decision-maker.

## 5.3 Management Reserve is not a default percentage

There is **no default 2%, 3%, 4%, 5% or other standard Management Reserve**.

The reserve is selected after reviewing:

1. quantified adverse sensitivities;
2. evidence-weighted expected exposures where supportable;
3. unresolved but unquantifiable tails;
4. correlation / overlap;
5. estimate maturity;
6. number and materiality of RED items;
7. client / contractual risk transfer;
8. confidence in labour, productivity, programme and quote completeness.

## 5.4 Recommended adjudication workflow

1. List every material RED / residual uncertainty.
2. Define the current priced baseline.
3. Define the adverse consequence or sensitivity where supportable.
4. Assess whether probability is:
   - supported;
   - management judgement; or
   - unknown.
5. Apply evidence-weighted exposure only where appropriate.
6. Check overlap and correlation.
7. Calculate the supportable aggregate weighted exposure.
8. Identify unquantified tails separately.
9. Select a Management Reserve amount/rate.
10. Record why that reserve is reasonable.
11. Freeze it as a **fixed amount for that estimate revision**.
12. Do not let the reserve automatically grow because other cost lines later change.
13. Re-adjudicate under formal change control when material information changes.

## 5.5 Consumption rule

When a RED resolves adversely:

**Do not automatically consume the Management Reserve and leave the estimate unchanged.**

Instead:

1. quantify the actual new consequence;
2. price it in the correct direct / PC / specialist / commercial cost location;
3. remove any superseded provisional treatment;
4. update Risk Truth;
5. reassess the remaining Management Reserve;
6. issue a controlled revision.

This preserves estimate truth, avoids double counting and supports future estimate-v-actual learning.

## 5.6 Programme treatment

Management Reserve is **not a programme task cost**.

Do not allocate it to Microsoft Project labour/plant/task Fixed Cost merely to make the programme equal the tender total.

Only real programme-related cost drivers belong in programme tasks.

# 6. LABOUR TRUE-COST POLICY

## 6.1 Principle

Labour resources must represent the cost to the contractor of employing and making that person available for productive project work.

Do not confuse:

- headline wage;
- salary;
- employee take-home pay;
- payroll withholding;
- employer direct cost;
- charge-out / sell rate.

The estimate normally requires **true direct employer cost**, unless the labour is being purchased as a quoted agency/subcontract resource.

## 6.2 Preferred evidence hierarchy

Use labour rates in this order where available:

### 1. Current company actual direct employment cost
Best source.

Use current payroll / employment records normalised to productive hours.

### 2. Reviewed company historical actual
Use where current actual is unavailable and the comparable is still representative.

### 3. Reviewed comparable contractor actual
Use as an independent benchmark.

### 4. External market wage / salary normalised to true employer cost
Use where company actual is unavailable.

Classify as AMBER until company actuals are available.

### 5. Raw advertised wage or salary
Do **not** use as a true-cost rate without normalisation.

---

# 7. TRUE EMPLOYMENT COST BUILD-UP

## 7.1 General formula

A labour rate should account for, where applicable:

**Gross wage / salary**  
+ **Employer statutory employment on-costs**  
+ **Employer retirement / superannuation contributions**  
+ **ACC / workers compensation / applicable employer levies**  
+ **Paid non-productive time recovered over productive hours**  
+ **Contractual employment allowances attributable to that employee**  
+ **Other direct employment costs not separately priced elsewhere**  
= **True direct employment cost**

Then normalise to:

**$/productive hour** or **$/productive day**

## 7.2 Paid non-productive time

Where the employee is paid but is not producing project work, the cost still exists and normally needs to be recovered over productive hours.

Consider, as applicable:

- annual leave;
- public holidays;
- reasonable sick/personal leave utilisation;
- statutory leave;
- paid training / inductions where normal;
- other paid non-productive time required by employment arrangements.

Do not automatically assume the maximum possible leave is consumed. Use reviewed actual experience where available.

## 7.3 NZ considerations

Typical NZ direct-employment cost considerations include:

- gross wage / salary;
- employer KiwiSaver contribution where applicable;
- ACC Work levy / applicable employer levy;
- annual leave;
- public holidays;
- reasonable sick leave utilisation;
- employment allowances where applicable.

**PAYE withheld from the employee is not an additional employer employment cost.** It is part of payroll administration of the employee's gross earnings.

## 7.4 Australian considerations

Typical AU direct-employment cost considerations include, where applicable:

- gross wage / salary;
- employer superannuation;
- workers compensation;
- payroll tax where applicable;
- annual leave;
- public holidays;
- personal leave;
- leave loading / allowances where contractually applicable.

Jurisdiction and employment arrangement must be recorded.

---

# 8. WHAT MUST NOT BE HIDDEN IN LABOUR

Keep the following separate unless there is a deliberate documented reason to include them in the labour resource:

- corporate overhead;
- head-office administration;
- tendering costs;
- profit;
- general company vehicle fleet;
- project preliminaries;
- project-specific accommodation/travel;
- overtime premium not actually required;
- plant operator already included in a wet-hire rate;
- PPE / tools / vehicle / phone where already included elsewhere.

A cost included in labour must not also be included in overhead, P&G, plant or another resource.

---

# 9. AGENCY / SUBCONTRACT LABOUR

Where labour is purchased externally:

Use the actual quoted / agreed charge to the contractor.

Confirm whether it includes:

- employment on-costs;
- agency margin;
- travel;
- vehicle;
- small tools;
- PPE;
- overtime;
- supervision;
- GST;
- mobilisation.

Do not add employer on-costs to a quoted agency/subcontract labour rate unless the contractor genuinely incurs them separately.

---

# 10. EXTERNAL MARKET NORMALISATION

Where only market wages/salaries are available:

1. Select relevant current market evidence.
2. Record:
   - role;
   - region;
   - date;
   - employment type;
   - wage/salary range;
   - source.
3. Choose a defensible gross-pay point.
4. Add applicable employer on-costs.
5. Convert paid annual cost into productive-hour cost.
6. Compare against:
   - another current market source;
   - a historical actual;
   - role hierarchy / first-principles reasonableness.
7. Classify the normalised rate AMBER unless it satisfies the full verification standard.

The Resource Rate Library description should state:

> **External market normalised direct employment cost benchmark — gross wage/salary converted to productive employer cost; excludes corporate overhead, profit and costs separately priced elsewhere.**

---

# 11. LABOUR RATE VERIFICATION STATUS

### GREEN
Current company actual or equivalent high-quality evidence, independently checked, and the productive-hour build-up is reproducible.

### AMBER
Normalised market / historical benchmark is reasonable but company actual is unavailable or one verification check remains incomplete.

### RED
Raw wage/salary has been used without a defensible true-cost conversion, or the employment basis / material inclusions are unknown.

---

# 12. RESOURCE LIBRARY RECORD REQUIREMENTS

Every reusable labour rate should retain:

- Resource Code;
- role;
- region / jurisdiction;
- employment basis;
- source date;
- base gross wage or salary basis;
- productive hours basis;
- employer on-cost assumptions;
- included costs;
- excluded costs;
- calculated true direct rate;
- evidence links;
- reviewer;
- verification status;
- effective date;
- superseded/replacement record where relevant.

Do not silently overwrite labour-rate history.

---

# 13. ESTIMATE ISSUE / RELEASE RULES

Before tender release:

1. Confirm material labour rates are true-cost rates or visibly AMBER proxies.
2. Confirm sell rates have not been substituted for direct costs.
3. Confirm wet-hire operator cost is not duplicated in labour.
4. Confirm labour overhead is not duplicated in company OH.
5. Confirm Management Reserve is separate from direct cost, Risk Truth, Weather, OH and profit.
6. Confirm any weighting used in Risk Truth has supportable evidence.
7. Confirm management-judgement probabilities are not misrepresented as verified.
8. Confirm all RED items remain visible until resolved, excluded or formally accepted.

---

# 14. CHANGE CONTROL

A controlled revision is required where:

- a material probability/weight changes;
- a RED becomes measurable;
- a Management Reserve is added, removed or changed;
- company actual labour costs replace market benchmarks;
- statutory employer on-costs materially change;
- a labour inclusion moves between Resource Rate, P&G or OH;
- any change affects programme/weather/risk integration.

On change:

1. update source evidence;
2. update verification status;
3. update Resource Rate Library where appropriate;
4. update Resource Build-Up / QS if direct cost changed;
5. update Risk Truth if risk treatment changed;
6. update Management Reserve adjudication;
7. reconcile Tender Build-Up / Summary / Cashflow;
8. regenerate programme outputs if cost/duration logic changed;
9. retain the previous revision for audit.

# 15. WORKED EXAMPLE — HOBSONVILLE REV34
## Example only — not a future default

This example demonstrates the method used to validate the policy.

### Current REDs

#### A. Rev17 vs Yodha earthworks
- Quantified adverse tender consequence: approximately **$28.6k sell**
- Current management judgement: adverse outcome **leans more likely than 50/50**
- Reason: latest Rev17 expressly updates site calculations, but Yodha remains the specialist civil mass-balance source
- Reasonable current adjudication range: approximately **55% to 65% adverse**
- Status of probability: **AMBER management judgement**, not verified Risk Truth probability

Weighted contribution range:
- 55% x $28.6k ≈ **$15.7k**
- 65% x $28.6k ≈ **$18.6k**

#### B. Ramp / entry / stairs ownership
- Current proxy adverse consequence: approximately **$9.5k sell minimum/floor**
- Ownership evidence does not currently favour Siteworks
- Reasonable current adjudication range: approximately **30% to 40% Siteworks adverse**
- Status: **AMBER management judgement**
- The impact itself is only a proxy pending current Rev17 remeasurement/build-up

Weighted contribution range:
- 30% x $9.5k ≈ **$2.9k**
- 40% x $9.5k ≈ **$3.8k**

#### C. Retaining 1.5m vs 1.8m
- Current architecture, structural and supplier evidence strongly favours the 1.8m profile
- However current blocks/no-fines quantities already include height bands to 1.8m
- Known incremental consequence: approximately **$0**
- Do not add a weighted dollar merely because probability is high
- Residual issue remains design coordination / method

#### D. PV01 concrete productivity
- Current labour budget is approximately equal to a 6-person crew x 10 days
- This makes a major direct-labour adverse outcome less likely than the earlier unreconciled view suggested
- Specialist methodology / ancillary tail remains unquantified
- Do not invent a weighted dollar until Q-05 or other evidence supports the impact

### Weighted known exposure

Using the above current management ranges, the quantified weighted sell exposure is approximately:

**$18.6k to $22.4k**

The midpoint is approximately:

**$20.5k**

Rev34 Management Reserve tender effect:

**approximately $22.4k**

### Hobsonville conclusion

The 4.0% Management Reserve is therefore consistent with the **upper end of the currently quantifiable weighted exposure range**, while still recognising that some tails remain unquantified.

It is reasonable for Rev34.

It is **not** a permanent 4% rule.

If Q-05, RFI-15, ramp ownership or another material input changes, the reserve must be re-adjudicated under change control.

# 16. POLICY CONCLUSION

The system shall distinguish four different things:

1. **Truth Estimate** — best evidence-based priced baseline.
2. **Risk Truth** — evidence-supported treatment/reconciliation of identified risks.
3. **Management Reserve** — management-held adjudication provision for aggregate residual tender-stage uncertainty not otherwise truthfully costed.
4. **Commercial Return** — overhead and profit, separately identified.

Likewise, labour shall distinguish:

1. **Headline wage / salary**
2. **True direct employment cost**
3. **Company overhead**
4. **Sell / charge-out rate**

These categories must never be silently interchanged.

**Absolute rule: expose uncertainty, price what can be priced, weight what can be supported, and never manufacture precision where the evidence does not exist.**
