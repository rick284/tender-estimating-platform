# 3Bears Estimating Knowledge Loop Standard — Rev1

**Date:** 9 September 2026  
**Status:** APPROVED PERMANENT INSTRUCTION  
**Applies to:** All 3Bears estimating chats, projects, estimates, variations, tender reviews, feasibility studies, rate build-ups, programme work and estimating-system development across New Zealand and Australia.

## 1. The Three Permanent Rules

### Rule 1 — Always Search for Knowledge

The estimating system must continuously search for better construction knowledge.

Before researching externally for an estimate, search the **3Bears Construction Intelligence Register** first.

When useful evidence is missing, stale, incomplete or non-comparable, research further using the strongest available lawful sources.

Useful knowledge includes, but is not limited to:

- current supplier and subcontractor pricing
- labour, plant and material costs
- cartage, disposal, fuel and statutory charges
- task productivity and crew outputs
- methodology and constructability
- programme and cycle-time evidence
- temporary works
- testing and compliance
- standards and regulation
- market capacity and escalation signals
- books, manuals, professional guidance, papers, courses and case studies
- reviewed company/project actuals

Knowledge collection is continuous. The system is never considered finished.

### Rule 2 — Always Improve

Every estimate, project and completed job must be treated as an opportunity to improve the next one.

Improvement must be evidence-based and version controlled.

Never silently overwrite history merely because newer evidence exists.

When stronger evidence is found:

1. retain the previous record;
2. record the new evidence;
3. normalise it;
4. verify it;
5. classify it GREEN / AMBER / RED;
6. supersede the old record only where justified;
7. record the reason and source;
8. preserve the prior tender assumption and historic context.

The objective is progressively less unsupported judgement, not false certainty.

### Rule 3 — Goal: Be the Best

The goal is to build the best construction estimating system we can reasonably create.

“Best” does not mean cheapest, highest, fastest, or most automated.

It means the system should continuously move toward being:

- more truthful
- more accurate
- more complete
- more evidence-based
- more current
- more context-aware
- more auditable
- more reproducible
- better at identifying uncertainty
- better at explaining cost and programme drivers
- better calibrated by real outcomes
- harder to mislead with weak or non-comparable evidence
- more useful to an experienced estimator without replacing estimator judgement

Every development decision should be tested against that objective.

## 2. The Mandatory Two-Way Knowledge Rule

Every estimate is both:

1. a **consumer of the Knowledge Bank**, and
2. a **producer of new Knowledge Bank evidence**.

The flow is mandatory:

**Knowledge Bank → Estimate → Verification / New Evidence → Knowledge Bank**

and, after construction:

**Estimate → Construction → Actual → Variance → Cause → Reviewed Actual → Knowledge Bank → Better Next Estimate**

There must be no one-way estimating workflow where useful evidence is verified inside a project and then disappears when that project closes.

## 3. Before Building an Estimate — Pull from the Knowledge Bank

Before researching or inserting external rates, productivity, methodology, supplier, standards, escalation, disposal, cartage, market or programme inputs:

1. Search the **3Bears Construction Intelligence Register**.
2. Identify current relevant records.
3. Check geography, date, scope, specification, units, tax basis, delivery/hire basis, crew/plant context, productivity conditions and validity.
4. Reuse only evidence that is genuinely comparable.
5. If evidence is stale, incomplete or unsuitable, research externally.
6. Record any important unresolved evidence gap.

The central register is a backend intelligence source only. It must not silently overwrite the live project estimate.

## 4. During the Estimate — Capture What We Learn

Whenever estimating work verifies, disproves, improves or materially contextualises an input, preserve that evidence.

This includes:

- supplier material prices
- subcontractor quotes
- labour rates
- plant hire rates
- owned-plant cost evidence
- fuel and surcharge evidence
- cartage rates
- disposal/gate rates
- statutory levies
- productivity rates
- crew compositions
- plant/crew combinations
- programme outputs
- cycle times
- construction methods
- temporary-works approaches
- test/inspection requirements
- standards or regulatory interpretations
- quote inclusions/exclusions
- minimum charges
- delivery/freight terms
- lead times
- project-specific conditions that explain why a rate or productivity differs
- rejected or contradicted evidence that would be dangerous to reuse later

A rate should never become more reusable by losing the context that created it.

## 5. Every Material Verified Rate Must Return to the Knowledge Bank

**Every material rate that is meaningfully verified during an estimate must be logged back into the central Knowledge Bank.**

This applies whether the rate ultimately becomes GREEN, AMBER or RED.

The purpose is not to promote every project rate as a universal company rate.

The purpose is to preserve what was learned.

For each returned rate, record as far as evidence allows:

- project / estimate reference
- item and specification
- supplier / subcontractor / company source
- source date
- quote or invoice reference
- location / region
- unit
- currency
- GST / tax basis
- supply-only / installed / wet hire / dry hire basis
- operator / fuel basis
- freight / delivery / cartage
- minimums / mobilisation
- quantity break or project volume
- inclusions
- exclusions
- validity / expiry
- first-principles check
- independent check
- evidence status
- limitations
- whether the rate is reusable, project-specific, historical, superseded or challenge-only

### Critical distinction — Log is not the same as Promote

A rate can and should be **logged** because it is useful evidence without being **promoted** to an active reusable rate.

Promotion to central Rate Intelligence requires the normal verification and comparability rules.

Examples:

- A project-specific discounted supplier quote may be valuable evidence but remain project-specific.
- A rate with incomplete freight basis may be AMBER.
- A stale but useful historic rate may remain HISTORICAL REFERENCE.
- A contradicted rate may be retained RED so the system knows not to reuse it.
- A GREEN project quote still does not become a universal rate unless its reuse conditions are understood.

Never average unlike rates merely to create a convenient benchmark.

## 6. Productivity and Method Knowledge Must Also Return

The same two-way rule applies to productivity and methodology.

If an estimate establishes or materially improves a production assumption, record it in the Knowledge Bank with:

- task
- output unit and period
- crew composition
- plant
- paid/shift hours
- productive/net hours where known
- gross elapsed versus net task time
- dimensions / depth / size
- complexity
- access and staging
- ground/site conditions
- material handling
- weather exposure
- testing / hold points
- work-front arrangement
- source context
- estimate use
- verification status

Never return a naked production number.

## 7. Supplier and Subcontractor Quote Return Rule

Project quotes are a major intelligence source.

At estimate issue or project closeout:

1. identify material project quotes received;
2. preserve the quote/source reference;
3. normalise the commercial basis;
4. record important inclusions, exclusions, validity and lead time;
5. log the resulting evidence in the central register;
6. classify whether it is:
   - project-specific only,
   - reusable benchmark,
   - current supplier market evidence,
   - historical reference,
   - superseded,
   - or unsuitable.

Commercially sensitive or private information must remain controlled.

Where necessary, store a sanitised central record while retaining the original protected source privately.

## 8. Estimate Knowledge Return Gate

Before an estimate workstream is considered internally complete, perform a **Knowledge Return Check**.

Answer:

1. Did we verify any new material rates?
2. Did we receive any useful supplier or subcontractor quotes?
3. Did we establish or improve any productivity assumptions?
4. Did we learn anything material about crew, plant, methodology, sequencing or temporary works?
5. Did we identify any standards, compliance or statutory-cost change?
6. Did we find evidence that supersedes or contradicts an existing central record?
7. Did we open any important research gaps?
8. Has all reusable or cautionary evidence been returned to the central register?
9. Has the original project evidence/source been retained?
10. Is the evidence correctly classified rather than promoted merely because it was used in an estimate?

A client tender may still be issued where the estimate itself passes the governing release gate and the commercial deadline requires issue.

However, the estimating workstream is not considered fully closed until the Knowledge Return Check is completed.

If failure to return evidence also means the live estimate lacks traceability, then normal RED/HOLD release rules still apply.

## 9. Post-Construction Return Rule

Where actual project information becomes available, return reviewed actuals to the Knowledge Bank.

Capture, where practical:

- estimated quantity vs actual quantity
- estimated rate vs actual paid cost
- estimated productivity vs actual productivity
- planned crew/plant vs actual crew/plant
- productive days vs working days vs elapsed days
- planned vs actual cartage/disposal
- weather effect
- risk outcome
- supplier/subcontractor performance
- cause of variance
- whether the estimating decision was reasonable on the evidence available at tender
- reuse conditions

Do not overwrite the original tender assumption.

The difference between estimate and actual is evidence.

## 12. Permanent Operating Statement

> **Every estimate must leave the estimating system smarter than it found it.**

The estimate consumes the best available evidence.

The estimate tests that evidence against a real project.

New verified evidence is returned to the Knowledge Bank.

Completed-project actuals calibrate the evidence further.

The next estimate starts from a stronger position.

That loop is permanent.
